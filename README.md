# 3DCC™
## The Three-Dimensional Cognitive Coordinate

**You are not looking at the map.  
You are inside it.**

3DCC is an experimental spatial interface for navigating human perception.

Instead of placing the observer outside a map, looking down at information, 3DCC places the observer at the origin:

**@ = YOU**

From that point, three perceptual axes extend into space and divide the field into 27 cognitive zones.

Objects are not presented as a list.

They exist as positions, distances, directions, and relationships around the observer.

---

## The idea

Most information interfaces place data in front of us.

Tables.  
Lists.  
Charts.  
Maps.

The observer remains outside the system.

3DCC changes the point of view.

The observer becomes the coordinate origin.

The world is then organized relative to that human point of perception.

This is why the third dimension matters.

Three axes do not simply add depth to a diagram. They create a navigable space around the observer — a space that can be entered, explored, rotated, and reached into.

> **3DCC is a cognitive reach.**

---

## The first implementation: cocktails

The first working implementation of 3DCC maps cocktails in three perceptual dimensions.

### Y — Structure
**Hard ↕ Light**

### X — Mouthfeel
**Crisp ↔ Creamy**

### Z — Sweetness
**Dry ↔ Sweet**

Three axes × three levels create:

**3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cognitive zones**

Each zone contains cocktails that occupy a similar perceptual position.

The current dataset includes a larger underlying cocktail map, while the public interface can present representative drinks for each zone.

Cocktails are the first demonstration.

They are not the destination.

---

## Enter @

The center of 3DCC is marked by **@**.

@ is not another data point.

It represents the observer.

Clicking @ changes the perspective from an external view of the coordinate to an internal one.

The viewer enters the origin.

From inside the coordinate, the surrounding spheres become perceptual targets distributed through three-dimensional space.

There is no visible @ anymore.

Because **you are @**.

---

## Reach

Select a sphere from inside the coordinate.

A neon vector travels from the observer's position to the selected object.

This is not only a selection animation.

It represents the central proposition of 3DCC:

**perception begins at the observer and reaches toward the world.**

The interface therefore makes the relationship between observer and object visible as space.

---

## Not a list. A field.

Three.js Daily described the project this way:

> **“the drinks sit in a field, not a list.”**

That distinction is central to 3DCC.

A list primarily expresses order.

A field can express:

- proximity
- distance
- direction
- neighborhood
- opposition
- transition
- relationship to the observer

3DCC explores whether those spatial relationships can become a new kind of interface for perception.

---

## Built with Three.js

The current browser prototype is built with **Three.js**.

Three.js is not being used here only for visual decoration or a 3D background.

It functions as the interface itself.

The user rotates the perceptual space, enters the origin, selects spatial objects, and experiences their relation to the observer directly.

No installation is required.

The experience runs in the browser.

---

## Why cocktails?

Cocktails provide a useful first test case because they are simultaneously:

- structured
- sensory
- culturally recognizable
- multidimensional
- difficult to reduce to a single hierarchy

A Martini and a Negroni are not simply entries in a database.

They occupy different perceptual positions.

3DCC asks whether those differences can be experienced spatially rather than merely described verbally.

---

## Beyond cocktails

The underlying concept is category-independent.

The same cognitive-coordinate approach may be explored for fields such as:

**Wine · Sake · Food · Fragrance · Music · Visual culture · Other perceptual domains**

The axes would change.

The principle would not.

**The observer remains the origin.**
