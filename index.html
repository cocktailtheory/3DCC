III — Originality

To divide in three dimensions —
the idea, and those who came before.

Colour spaces that quantify hue; psychological models that chart emotion. The attempt to cast vague sensation into three dimensions is a century old. But those were difficult instruments, used by researchers behind the scenes. A grown-up puzzle you could turn, search, and play with — for tonight’s glass — existed nowhere.

IV — Relational Fidelity

3DCC does not write the taste.
It writes the taste’s relations.

A coordinate does not mark a position. It is a language for preserving relations. Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka — however the notation of their addresses changes, the relative positions of the three do not. A coordinate is a way of describing the world without breaking it.

A map is not the world made smaller. It discards distance and lets relation rise to the surface. The subway diagram is the emblem of this.† It shows neither the distance between stations nor the true angle of the lines. And still, no one loses their way. What the rider needs is not the correct position but the correct relation — where to get off, where to change.

3DCC stands on the same principle. It records no recipe. So long as the relation holds — where Sidecar lies in relation to Alexander — the drinker can find the way back to yesterday’s glass.

To this idea — of writing relation faithfully — I gave a name.
Relational Fidelity.

† In mathematics, the view that discards distance and angle to keep only how things connect is called topology. Harry Beck’s 1931 London Underground map — drawn by throwing distance away — is its most famous instance. Relational Fidelity is, in a sense, the topology of taste: the preservation of a flavour’s arrangement as topologically identical. Not precision, but arrangement. Not the taste itself, but the space between one taste and another. That is what 3DCC draws.

V — The Vessel

This is the invention of a vessel.

The three axes given to the cocktail mark the basic bearings. But the mission of 3DCC lies one level above that dimension — to govern the vessel itself. To grasp preference in three dimensions, seat the subject at the origin, and turn the space to search and draw out. Change the axes, and music, scent, film may be caught in the same coordinate system.

Yet the beauty of the vessel alone reveals nothing. Only with the right three axes and the right choice of items does the space acquire meaning. So 3DCC first proves its quality at a single origin — the cocktail.

VI — How it differs · analyse vs. coordinate

Not classification. Coordinate.

Not decomposition, but relation.

Wheels, radars, even the “AI that puts taste into words” — existing methods analyse and decompose a single object. 3DCC coordinates many objects and shows their relations. And it lets you turn the whole, and pick out one glass.
